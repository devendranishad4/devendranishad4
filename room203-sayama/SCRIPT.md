# ROOM 203 — recording script
A Japanese apartment horror episode in the original Sayama v0.3 city. You play the new tenant on the second floor. The neighbour in the supposedly empty Room 201 has followed previous tenants. Aim for 8–12 minutes with your spoken exploration; travel and interactions control the pace. The story does not force an exact video duration.

## 1. Arrival — street to Room 203 (roughly 1–2 minutes of footage)
Run `/room203 auto` and close chat. The 20-second delay is your recording preparation, not part of the story. Start your shot on the rainy street. Show the apartment exterior, the narrow lane and the staircase. Walk toward the small objective, climb the real stairs and find door 203. Open it and step inside. Rain gives way to a low building hum as you enter.

Voice: “Japan mein mera pehla apartment… Room 203. Rent itna kam tha ki maine questions hi nahi pooche. Building purani hai, par ghar theek lag raha hai. Bas yeh corridor… kuch zyada hi quiet hai.”

Show the existing interior and inspect your room. Avoid racing directly to the chest; let your commentary establish that you live here.

## 2. Lease note — bedside chest
Right-click the bedside chest when the objective asks. This is a story interaction, not a fuse collection. The note appears once, below the small objective, for 18 seconds:

“Room 201 is empty. Keep your door closed after midnight.”

Voice: “201 khaali hai… phir landlord ne specifically uska naam kyun likha? Aur midnight ke baad door band rakhna? Theek hai. Main koi hero nahi ban raha.”

## 3. The first minute — listen inside
Stay inside 203. From the chest interaction, the director plays footsteps overhead at 20 seconds, three knocks at your door at 34 seconds, footsteps down the hallway at 38 seconds, and three more knocks at 50 seconds. At 60 seconds the objective changes to “Check your front door.” Sounds come from their actual locations.

At the overhead steps: “Upar koi walk kar raha hai? Landlord ne kaha tha yahan zyada log nahi rehte.”
At the first knocks: stop talking for the three knocks. “Kaun hai? …Hello? Naam toh bolo.”
At the hallway steps: “Awaaz door se door ja rahi hai. Koi jawab kyun nahi de raha?”
At the second knocks: hold your camera on the door. “Wahi teen knocks. Main ab dekhunga.”

Right-click the front door after the objective changes. The next scene starts from this interaction, so an early click does not skip the buildup.

## 4. The empty room's neighbour
Step into the corridor and look toward Room 201, up the hallway. One second after this scene starts, the neighbour appears near its door. You hear a breath. When you look toward him with a clear line of sight, a short sting plays and he disappears. If you never look, the scene advances after 20 seconds so recording cannot become stuck.

Voice: “Bhai… 201 ke bahar koi khada hai. Face… woh meri taraf dekh raha hai? Hello?” Then react to the disappearance: “Abhi yahin tha! Main camera move bhi nahi kiya…”

Return inside 203. That movement triggers the light failure and the next note. The door closes behind you; you can open it again normally.

## 5. The upstairs clue — second floor to third floor
Note: “The tenant in 303 saw him too. His diary is still upstairs.”

Voice: “303 wala bhi ise dekh chuka hai. Shayad diary mein kuch hoga. Mujhe bas stairs tak pahunchna hai.”

Open your door, turn toward the stair landing and follow the objectives to the upper stair entrance. The iron stair door is already opened by the director. Climb the U-shaped staircase, open the wooden landing door and walk to Room 303. Open its room door and right-click the chest.

Take this journey slowly enough to film the original corridor, railing and real stairs. No new buildings are generated and no lift is involved.

## 6. Diary — the final warning
The diary text appears once:

“He copies the footsteps above you. Three knocks means he knows which room is yours. Leave before he reaches your door.”

Voice: “Woh footsteps copy karta hai… Matlab upar koi tenant tha hi nahi? Teen knocks ka matlab usse mera room pata hai. Main yahan nahi rukne wala.”

Five seconds after opening the diary chest, three knocks sound at 303. At eight seconds the neighbour appears in the third-floor corridor and begins following you. The objective says to run downstairs. The short diary interval is intentional urgency: read the longer voiceover during editing if necessary.

## 7. Escape — use the same real stairwell
Leave 303, head along the corridor to the landing, go down the upper stairs, then down the original ground staircase to the street. A pulsing pursuit sound plays; breath is audible when the neighbour gets close. The actor follows with Minecraft pathfinding and can open wooden doors, but does not injure you or teleport you. The HUD guides the route across landings rather than pointing through a wall to the final exit.

Voice: “Woh bahar hai! Stairs, stairs… door khula hai. Neeche jaana hai. Peechhe nahi dekhna!”

When you reach the street exit at 682 30 -150, the actor is removed, the indoor dim overlay lifts, and the objective says to stay outside and listen.

## 8. Street ending — do not cut immediately
Hold the exterior shot. At 15 seconds, three knocks sound close by. At 23 seconds, the neighbour is visible near the apartment street and a final sting plays. At 32 seconds the episode ends and the actor is removed.

Voice after the knocks: “Main building se bahar aa gaya… phir knocks kahan se aa rahe hain?”
Final line: “Shayad woh Room 203 mein nahi rehta. Shayad ab woh mere saath hai.”

Hold 3–5 seconds, then cut to black in your video editor. The addon displays “Episode complete.” Run `/room203 stop` after filming to restore the previous environment and game mode. `/room203 reset` gives another take; `/room203 restore` also restores all changed original blocks.

## Practical filming
Use first-person view and the right-corner objective while navigating. Use `/room203 hud off` only when you know the route and want clean footage; `/room203 hud on` restores guidance. The script assumes you follow the stated route and interactions. Do not fly, teleport, break doors or leave the building during the first minute. Player movement drives the scenes, so exploration and narration can stretch the episode naturally.
